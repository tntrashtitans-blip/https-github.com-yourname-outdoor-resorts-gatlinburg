RV lot vs. Smoky Mountain cabin: which investment actually cash flows?

Back to all posts

Home › Investing › RV lot vs. cabin

Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg · Investment Guide

Cabins dominate the Smoky Mountain search results. An RV lot inside an established resort is easier to overlook, and easier to misunderstand. This page compares the two purchases as different assets: what you would own, what a guest would be renting, and which costs belong on a worksheet you fill in with numbers you can verify. It does not predict cash flow, and it does not quote a price.

What each purchase actually is

A Smoky Mountain cabin, in the usual listing, is a house. You are buying a residential structure, the land interest under it, and whatever permits and rental rules apply to that house. Financing, insurance, and resale often follow house patterns. The guest is booking bedrooms, a kitchen, and the cabin's own setting.

An Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg lot is a site inside a 376-site owner association that began in 1970. The association's site says every site has full hookups: 30-amp and 50-amp electric, water, sewer, cable TV, and Wi-Fi. Some sites can take trailers up to 42 feet. If a camper is included, that camper is a second asset. The 1031 exchange guide explains why that split matters if you are moving sale proceeds. Read it as education, then ask your own CPA.

What the guest is renting

Cabin guests and RV guests are not always the same household. A cabin search is a house search. A Gatlinburg RV rental is a furnished camper or park model already on a lot, or a vacant lot for people who bring their own rig. You can see both on the rentals page. The stay guide tells guests what to confirm: sleep count, hookups, parking, pets, and the fact that tent camping is not offered. The Gatlinburg RV resort guide puts the association's amenity list next to that booking path. If you are thinking about owning, read those pages first. They describe the stay you would be offering.

Line the costs up before you compare them

A blog that prints a purchase price, a nightly rate, or a debt payment is guessing the day you read it. Use a current listing, a lender, an insurer, and the association for the figures. The useful comparison is which lines exist.

The constraints that change the math

Season is the first constraint, for cabins and for RVs. Holiday weeks, summer, and festival weekends behave differently from a quiet January. Build the year in seasons, and leave the empty weeks visible.

The association adds a constraint a standalone cabin may not have. The restated master deed on orgtn.net excludes ground tents. It also describes a three-week maximum for a non-owner occupant, then at least fourteen days away before that guest stays again. Owners and immediate family are treated differently in that section. If your model assumes a renter can occupy the site for months, check the current deed. A cabin turnover is a house turnover. An RV turnover is a smaller interior with slides, a roof, tanks, and RV appliances. Price that list with an RV tech.

Personal nights are not rent on either worksheet. Write them down before you call the rest occupancy. Pet rules, pool season, and parking in the stay guide are part of what a listing has to explain.

Where a cabin is the clearer fit

Choose a cabin when the guest you want is a cabin guest. Larger groups, a residential kitchen, and a conventional house loan are cabin questions. So is resale to people shopping for houses. A cabin can also be the right answer when you do not want association stay limits or RV rules on top of the rental. That does not make a cabin automatically more profitable. It makes the product match a house-shaped trip.

Where an ORG lot is the clearer fit

Choose the lot when the guest you want is an RV guest, and when you want the association's setting to do part of the explaining. Pools, the lake, the creeks, mini-golf, and the East Parkway location are on the association's own description. A furnished rental borrows that setting. A vacant lot borrows it for someone who already owns the camper.

The lot path is also the one to study if the check you can write is for a site rather than a house, or if you already own an RV and you are comparing storage with a site you might also rent. The storage comparison is that second case. Financing may be nothing like a house mortgage. Ask a lender what they will fund before you fall in love with a layout on the lot map.

The cash-flow test, using your numbers

Run the same steps for a cabin you can tour and for a site you can tour. Compare the finished worksheets. Skip any version that arrives with someone else's rates already filled in.

  1. Write the monthly carrying cost you can document: a loan payment if a lender offered one, insurance, taxes, association assessments, utilities you would pay, platform fees, supplies, a repair reserve, and professional fees.
  2. Write nightly rates you can support from listings that are live today, split by season.
  3. Divide that month's carrying cost by a cautious nightly rate. The result is nights needed before the month covers those costs. It is not profit.
  4. Subtract the nights you want to use yourself.
  5. Check the constraint. For an ORG site, that includes the association's rules for non-owner stays. For a cabin, it includes the permit and rental rules for that house.

A lower purchase price can mean a lower carrying cost. It can also mean a lower ceiling on the nightly rate and a stricter rule on how long a renter may stay. Both belong on the worksheet.

Questions before you compare a lot and a cabin

Does an RV lot at Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg cash-flow better than a cabin?

There is no general answer. A lower price can mean fewer nights to cover costs, and an RV stay can also rent for less while following association stay limits. Compare two worksheets built from current documents.

What am I buying if the listing includes a camper?

A site in the association, plus a camper. Ask how the contract splits the price, how the camper is titled, and which piece a lender will finance.

What can a guest actually book?

A furnished RV or park model, or a vacant lot if they bring a camper. Tent camping is not offered. See the rentals page and the stay guide.

Are association dues and rental rules the same as a cabin HOA?

Each property has its own documents. Read the restated master deed on orgtn.net, including the limit on non-owner stays, and ask the seller for the current assessment.

Who should I talk to before making an offer?

A qualified professional for tax, legal, and lending questions, and the association or the seller for current rules and assessments. This page is general education, not tax, legal, or financial advice.
