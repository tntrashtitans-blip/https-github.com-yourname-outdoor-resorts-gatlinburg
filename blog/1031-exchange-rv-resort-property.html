1031 exchanges, bonus depreciation, and RV resort property

A 1031 exchange can let you sell investment real estate and move the proceeds into other investment real estate while deferring the capital-gains tax on that sale. The gain is deferred, not erased. If the replacement property is later sold in a taxable sale, the earlier gain is still part of the story unless another valid deferral applies.

People looking at Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg sometimes ask whether a lot, or a lot with a camper already on it, can be that replacement property. What Section 1031 actually covers

Section 1031 applies when real property held for business or investment is exchanged for like-kind real property that will be held the same way. The IRS explains the current rule in its like-kind exchange tax tips.

For exchanges completed after December 31, 2017, like-kind treatment is limited to real property. Inside real property, like-kind is still broad: investment land can replace other investment real estate. It does not mean cabin for cabin. Both the property sold and the property bought have to be held for business or investment.

Whether a site at Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg is real property in your state, and whether you will hold it for investment, is a documents question. The association's site, orgtn.net, describes a resort that began in 1970, with 376 individually owned sites. That ownership is why a lot can show up in a real-estate conversation. It does not, by itself, answer the tax question. This directory is not orgtn.net, and it does not prepare exchanges.

The camper is a different asset from the lot

Sales here are often described as one package: the site and the camper parked on it. Tax law splits that package. The land interest is the part that might be real property. A towable RV, a motorhome, and the furniture inside it are generally personal property. Personal property is not like-kind replacement property under the post-2017 rule.

If one price covers both, the price has to be allocated. Only the real-property portion can serve as replacement property. Value assigned to the camper, to furnishings, or taken as cash can be taxable boot, even when the lot itself belongs in the exchange. A lot-and-camper purchase can still be sensible. It is a poor thing to treat as one undivided 1031 asset.

The clock, and why it does not move

In a delayed exchange, a qualified intermediary holds the money. If you receive the sale proceeds yourself, the exchange generally fails. The intermediary has to be engaged before the old property closes.

  1. Before closing, the intermediary is under contract and the proceeds go to that intermediary.
  2. Day 0 is the transfer. Both deadlines run from that day, including when the last day is a weekend or a holiday.
  3. By midnight of day 45, you identify replacement property in a signed writing, delivered the way the intermediary requires.
  4. You receive the replacement property by the earlier of day 180 or the due date of your federal return for that year, including extensions. Day 180 does not restart at day 45.

How many properties you can name

Under the three-property rule you can identify up to three properties of any value. Under the 200 percent rule you can identify more than three if their combined fair market value is not more than twice what you sold. Go beyond that, and you generally have to close on at least 95 percent of the value of everything you identified. Have the lot-versus-camper allocation ready before you sign the identification notice.

Investment intent and personal use

Replacement property has to be held for investment or for use in a business. Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg is also a place owners visit. Personal use can exist. It is also the fact that makes a 1031 file messy when nobody writes the plan down.

The IRS safe harbor in Revenue Procedure 2008-16 is for dwelling units. In general it looks at the 12 months before the exchange and the 12 months after: own the dwelling the whole time, rent it at a fair rental for at least 14 days in each period, and keep personal use to the greater of 14 days or 10 percent of the fair-rental days. It is a safe harbor, not the only possible facts, and a campsite with a camper may not be the dwelling it describes. Keep a calendar of rented nights, the rent, and the nights you blocked. Ask a CPA whether it applies.

Bonus depreciation is a second conversation

Land is not depreciable. A camper or furnishings might be, if the asset is eligible and placed in service in a rental or business use. That question is separate from whether the lot is 1031 replacement property. One answer can be yes and the other no in the same closing.

On January 14, 2026, the IRS issued Notice 2026-11 guidance on the One, Big, Beautiful Bill. The IRS describes a permanent 100 percent additional first-year depreciation deduction for eligible property acquired after January 19, 2025. Taxpayers may elect a smaller percentage, or elect out, in the situations the notice covers. Acquisition date, placed-in-service date, business-use percentage, and the kind of asset all matter. A park model on a permanent setup can be analyzed differently from a towable RV. A state return may not follow the federal rule. Ask the CPA to allocate the price and say which pieces, if any, qualify.

Passive activity, in plain language

IRS Publication 925 covers passive activity losses. Real estate professional status generally means more than half your personal services for the year are in real property trades or businesses where you materially participate, and those hours exceed 750. A short-stay rule also matters: an activity is generally outside the rental-activity definition when the average period of customer use is seven days or less, so material participation can matter even without real estate professional status. Whether a loss offsets wages is the CPA's conclusion after they see the year.

Questions people ask before they call a CPA

Can an RV lot at Outdoor Resorts Gatlinburg be 1031 replacement property?

It can be the real-property part of a purchase if you hold that part for investment or business use and the rest of the exchange rules are met. The camper and furnishings are generally personal property, so they are not like-kind replacement property. A CPA and a qualified intermediary apply that split to your contract.

Does a lot-and-camper package count as one exchange asset?

The price has to be allocated. Value that is not like-kind real property can be taxable boot even when the lot is part of a valid exchange.

What are the 1031 deadlines?

In a delayed exchange you generally have 45 days from the sale to identify replacement property in writing, and you must receive it by the earlier of 180 days or your tax-return due date, including extensions. Engage the qualified intermediary before the sale closes.

Does personal use of a Gatlinburg lot ruin a 1031?

Personal use is a facts question. Revenue Procedure 2008-16 is one IRS safe harbor for dwelling units, and a resort site may not fit it. Keep a calendar and ask a tax professional.

Does bonus depreciation apply to the camper?

Land does not qualify. The IRS describes a permanent 100 percent additional first-year deduction for eligible property acquired after January 19, 2025. Whether a camper or furnishing is eligible is a CPA question, separate from the 1031.
